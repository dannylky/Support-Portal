🔍 Observations

  1. The problem set is the display chain. 152 of 167 cases (91.0%) are Classroom – Display; every other category combined is 15. Investment and root-cause effort belong in the AVoIP display path — panels, decoders, TVs and their power/signal behaviour — not in general classroom IT.
  2. Room 1 is the hotspot by a wide margin. 52 cases (31.1%), well ahead of Room 2 (33). It is also the largest room (four TVs) and the repeat room for panel flashing, so exposure and failure rate compound there.
  3. Flashing is the single biggest recurring theme. 64 cases (38.3%) under the two main flashing titles describe the same panel-flashing phenomenon. Counted as one programme rather than 64 incidents, it is the clearest mitigation target in the data.
  4. November shows a step change, not a drift. 92 cases in 27 days (≈3.4/day) after October's 19 in 31 days (≈0.6/day); the peak day, 6 Nov, alone produced 12 cases — 7.3% of the entire period's caseload, and 23 Nov added 10 more, the second-busiest day on record, with 26 Nov close behind at 9. Something changed at the start of November (term load, morning start-up pattern, reporting practice) and the data cannot separate those causes — but the rate itself is unambiguous.
  5. The active queue is the operational risk. 63 cases (37.7%) remain In Progress, under Observation, Open or Pending User, and “Open” appears only from November. Even if the underlying faults are minor, an unreviewed queue hides the few that are not.
  6. Signal beats power. Signal device counts (25) exceed TV power-on (14) and TV restart (14) counts, so encoder/decoder signal stability deserves at least as much attention as TV power behaviour — the same ordering the workbook's October diagnosis view showed for its month.
  7. The workbook's own dashboards no longer describe its data. The older dashboard's findings text still speaks of 57 cases and a 6-case peak day; the October-only diagnosis view has a broken daily table (#N/A). Anyone reading those sheets today is misled — the figures on this page are recomputed from the case list itself.

✅ Suggestions

  1. Run Room 1 as a root-cause programme. With close to a third of all cases, Room 1 should get the first dedicated review: panel-flash mitigation (the decoder display-behaviour delay change already identified), TV power behaviour, and its four-decoder chain — before equivalent effort is spread across other rooms.
  2. Manage flashing as one programme. One title taxonomy, one mitigation, one tracked burn-down — reviewed against the morning flash checks until they run clean, instead of a fresh case per morning.
  3. Triage the 63 active cases weekly, oldest first. Each gets an explicit close-or-observe decision; the November “Open” batch in particular should not age silently.
  4. Standardise the intake fields. A fixed Location list (SM-11-01…08 plus campus options), one definition for closed (merge “Done” into “Resolved”, or define the difference), and a controlled Case Title list. Location alone is currently written in at least six spellings for the same room, which is what makes reports like this one need manual normalisation.
  5. Retire or rebuild the in-workbook dashboards. Publish from the case list (as this page does) so the headline numbers can never drift a whole era behind the data again.
  6. Set a rate trigger. If daily intake stays above ~3 cases/day, escalate from per-case handling to a fleet-level review (firmware state, power schedule, panel behaviour) — November's rate already crosses that line.
