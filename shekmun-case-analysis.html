🗂️ Cases by category

One category dominates: Classroom – Display, 105 cases (87.5%). Software and PC Hardware contribute 5 each; System 2; User Guide, User Training and Event support 1 each. This is an AV display-chain caseload, not a general IT pattern.

🏫 Cases by room

Room 1 alone carries 43 cases (35.8%); Rooms 1 + 2 together are 66 (55%). A further 13 cases sit at campus or shared locations (11/F, lecterns, “all classrooms”). Locations are normalised from free text — see Data & method.

📌 Status mix

Resolved 69 and Done 10 give a 65.8% closure rate. The remaining 41 cases (34.2%) are In Progress (23), under Observation (8) or Open (10). Notably, the “Open” status appears only from November — the active queue is growing faster than it is being cleared.

🔌 Device incidents by type

Counted with the workbook's own keyword formulas over titles and descriptions (a case can count in more than one class): signal incidents lead with 25 device counts, ahead of TV power-on (14) and TV restart (14). The signal path — encoder / decoder — generates more device incidents than TV power behaviour.

🔍 Observations

  1. The problem set is the display chain. 105 of 120 cases (87.5%) are Classroom – Display; every other category combined is 15. Investment and root-cause effort belong in the AVoIP display path — panels, decoders, TVs and their power/signal behaviour — not in general classroom IT.
  2. Room 1 is the hotspot by a wide margin. 43 cases (35.8%), nearly double Room 2 (23). It is also the largest room (four TVs) and the repeat room for panel flashing, so exposure and failure rate compound there.
  3. Flashing is the single biggest recurring theme. 29 cases (24.2%) under two titles describe the same System ON flashing phenomenon. Counted as one programme rather than 29 incidents, it is the clearest mitigation target in the data.
  4. November shows a step change, not a drift. 45 cases in 14 days (≈3.2/day) after October's 19 in 31 days (≈0.6/day); the peak day, 6 Nov, alone produced 12 cases — 10% of the entire period's caseload. Something changed at the start of November (term load, morning start-up pattern, reporting practice) and the data cannot separate those causes — but the rate itself is unambiguous.
  5. The active queue is the operational risk. 41 cases (34.2%) remain In Progress, under Observation or Open, and “Open” appears only from November. Even if the underlying faults are minor, an unreviewed queue hides the few that are not.
  6. Signal beats power. Signal device counts (25) exceed TV power-on (14) and TV restart (14) counts, so encoder/decoder signal stability deserves at least as much attention as TV power behaviour — the same ordering the workbook's October diagnosis view showed for its month.
  7. The workbook's own dashboards no longer describe its data. The older dashboard's findings text still speaks of 57 cases and a 6-case peak day; the October-only diagnosis view has a broken daily table (#N/A). Anyone reading those sheets today is misled — the figures on this page are recomputed from the case list itself.

✅ Suggestions

  1. Run Room 1 as a root-cause programme. With over a third of all cases, Room 1 should get the first dedicated review: panel-flash mitigation (the decoder display-behaviour delay change already identified), TV power behaviour, and its four-decoder chain — before equivalent effort is spread across other rooms.
  2. Manage flashing as one programme. One title taxonomy, one mitigation, one tracked burn-down — reviewed against the morning flash checks until they run clean, instead of a fresh case per morning.
  3. Triage the 41 active cases weekly, oldest first. Each gets an explicit close-or-observe decision; the November “Open” batch in particular should not age silently.
  4. Standardise the intake fields. A fixed Location list (SM-11-01…08 plus campus options), one definition for closed (merge “Done” into “Resolved”, or define the difference), and a controlled Case Title list. Location alone is currently written in at least six spellings for the same room, which is what makes reports like this one need manual normalisation.
  5. Retire or rebuild the in-workbook dashboards. Publish from the case list (as this page does) so the headline numbers can never drift a whole era behind the data again.
  6. Set a rate trigger. If daily intake stays above ~3 cases/day, escalate from per-case handling to a fleet-level review (firmware state, power schedule, panel behaviour) — November's rate already crosses that line.

One case covers both Room 5 and Room 6 and is counted once in each room's bar, so room bars sum to 108 counts over 107 distinct room cases; the remaining 13 cases are campus/shared locations. Device counts use the workbook's own keyword formulas (TV Power-On / Signal Device / TV Restart counts); a case can score in more than one class.
Privacy. Published figures are statistics only. Reporter names (Reported By) and the free-text Case Description are excluded; case titles are published only as aggregate counts of recurring titles.
