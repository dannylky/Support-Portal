Observations

  1. Everything is source-side. Every loss inside buffer coverage has an encoder stop / no-source counterpart within 1–6 seconds. There is no decoder-side fault signature anywhere today: no link down/up, no thermal warnings, no service restarts, no loop residue.
  2. No decoder ever fails alone. In every room, decoders always share the same state — four together in R1, pairs together in R2/R7/R8, singles matching their own encoder in R3–R6. A single decoder fault would break this pattern; none did.
  3. Two time patterns, two meanings. The 18:30–18:59 recovery cluster (five rooms in 30 minutes) is a shared end-of-class pattern; the 20:55–21:48 shutdowns (spread over ~55 minutes) are individual lectern PCs switching off. Neither pattern looks like a network event — and the switch logs agree.
  4. Midday reboots are a warning, not a remedy. R1 and R2 decoders rebooted around 13:53 and 13:56 and still could not lock until the evening. If nobody directed those reboots, check the two rooms' power / PoE stability during the day. Either way, rebooting decoders should not be the first remedy for a source-side stop.
  5. R1's morning is the weakest link. Its lectern PC never fed normally in the morning — one brief start at 08:43:35, then nothing until the evening. Combined with its 9-hour daytime stop, R1's lectern PC is the first field-check candidate.
  6. USB storms are a standby phenomenon, not a daytime one. Daytime windows were quiet except two post-re-lock re-enumerations (R2 DEC-1 18:47, R7 DEC-1 18:30). In the evening all four known units storm again at standby rates similar to the morning. Storm logs are so dense (a 1,000-line buffer covers only ~12–15 minutes) that a storming unit's log alone cannot judge its stream state — PTZ / thumbnails must corroborate, as they did today.
  7. The Manager evidence path broke, the AVoIP did not. At 14:00 and 16:00 every thumbnail fetch failed on TLS handshake timeout to the Manager (172.18.22.5:443), even after manual retries — while PTZ (port 80) and P3000 capture worked normally. It recovered from 18:00. Recorded as thumbnail-unavailable, never as a device fault.
  8. Two minor anomalies, neither changes any room-level conclusion. R1 DEC-4 missed the brief 21:44 re-lock because the source was only up for 9 seconds — normal search-timing spread. R7's encoder returned only 71 lines at 22:00 (vs 1,000 at 18:00/20:00) — readable and feeding at the tail, but its pre-21:52 evening events cannot be verified from that run.
  9. Fleet health is otherwise clean. Discovery loop 0, storage pressure 0, LOG-ACTION stable at 1,3 everywhere, all clocks NTP-synced, no new error types, HDCP lines only following loss/re-lock events.
