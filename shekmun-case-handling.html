🛠️ ShekMun Campus — Case Handling for Known Issues

Scenario-by-scenario handling procedures for every known issue at Shek Mun, compiled from live operating experience since October 2026. Cases are ordered by importance — impact on teaching first, then how much harm a wrong action can do. Each case follows the same pattern: how it is recognised, the step-by-step handling, what not to do, and how it closes. How to use this page. Find the case that matches the symptoms, then follow its steps in order — the early steps are always read-only checks. Critical cases can stop a class or a whole morning and have pre-agreed handling; High cases affect a room's teaching directly; Medium cases are known issues with a defined remedy or a deliberate observe-only decision; the final case needs no action at all — it exists so nobody “fixes” a non-fault. Unless a case says otherwise, any device-changing step needs the site owner's confirmation first (SOP §8).

Case 1 Control panel lights flashing / unsettled Critical

Recognise it

A room's Extron panel buttons (System ON / TV On — Room 1 also Side TV On) do not settle: two state samples ~15 s apart disagree, or a press ends with the lights still flashing. Found by the 07:05 morning run, by patrol flash checks, or reported by a teacher.

Handle it — step by step

  1. Take two read-only samples ~15 s apart. Both settled-on → not this case. Same non-on state twice → stable off: report plainly as “TV off (stable, not flashing)” and stop — only a flashing button justifies a PCU reboot.
  2. If flashing is confirmed, check whether this is the 07:05 morning run or a patrol/other time — the handling differs (steps 3 vs 4).
  3. Morning run (Option A, pre-authorised): power-cycle the room's PCU-AVRack outlet 4 once (OFF → ~10 s → ON). Wait for the controller to boot and re-sync. If the room self-restores, finish. If it is stably OFF with TVs on, press System ON once. Confirm with two samples + PTZ. Still flashing after that → stop, report, ask the owner.
  4. Patrol / any other time: create one USS Help Desk record for the room (pre-approved for flashing cases) and note its CaseID in the report; then request the owner's approval for the PCU cycle — never perform it unapproved outside the morning run.
  5. Report the room first in the run's anomalies; flashing is an ABNORMAL criterion.
Do NOT: use PCU-Lectern outlets; cycle more than once per room per morning; propose a PCU reboot for a stably-off room.
Experience note: SM-11-01 is the repeat room (flashing on consecutive mornings, settled inside the run under Option A; its Help Desk record was filed and resolved the same day). Three consecutive flashing-free mornings retire the Option A pre-authorisation automatically.

Case 2 Room NOT READY before class — decoder / display-side fault Critical

Recognise it

Readiness Stage 2 judges a room NOT READY: the PC source is live, but a display is dark, a TV decoder is offline, or the AVoIP evidence shows the picture is not reaching the screens.

Handle it — step by step

  1. Confirm the fault is display/decoder-side, not source-side: PC encoder shows a live image (if not, go to Case 3).
  2. Gather evidence in the fixed order: AVoIP thumbnails → PTZ view → snapshot status/temperatures.
  3. Escalate to the owner in the main chat with the room, the evidence and the proposed remedy. Wait for confirmation — the readiness run never acts by itself.
  4. After confirmation, apply the room-group remedy once: Rooms 02/07/08 → “Reset all decoders” (Companion). Rooms 01/03/04/05/06 → restart that room's TV decoders one by one via P3000 #RESET (audio decoder excluded).
  5. Re-run the readiness evidence for the room and report READY or still NOT READY before the 09:00 class start.
Do NOT: press “Source: PC” as a remedy; reset the audio decoder; repeat the reset without reporting the first result.

Case 3 PC source NO SIGNAL / black screen — source-side High

Recognise it

The room's PC encoder shows NO SIGNAL or a black picture while decoders and displays are healthy. In reports this is 無視訊來源信號 (no video source signal) — a source problem, not a decoder fault.

Handle it — step by step

  1. Check the lectern PC is powered and past BIOS/boot (a sleeping or off PC is the most common cause).
  2. Check the Windows display mode: it must be Duplicate. In Extend mode the encoder is fed the blank extended desktop and the room shows black while everything “works”.
  3. Check the HDMI lead into the encoder is seated, and the encoder has PoE/network.
  4. If the PC wakes and the picture returns by the next scheduled round, record it (see Case 12 if it fits the late-wake pattern) — no device action needed.
  5. If the source stays dead into class time, escalate to the owner with the checks already done; an on-site PC check is the usual next step.
Do NOT: reset decoders or the AV processor for a source-side fault — it cannot help and disrupts the room.
Experience note: the 2026-10-15 Room 7 NOT READY was exactly this case — Windows was in Extend mode. It is now the first thing checked in every PC-source-black case.

Case 4 Multi-display out of sync High

Recognise it

In a multi-display room (SM-11-01, 02, 07, 08) the screens disagree: one dark/off while others show content or standby. Judged from PTZ + decoder status together.

Handle it — step by step

  1. Room 1 exception first: in SM-11-01, any one TV off/dark while any other is on = OUT OF SYNC / ABNORMAL immediately — no second-run wait. All four off together is normal uniform off, not this case.
  2. Other rooms: confirm across two consecutive patrol runs before calling it out of sync (a single frame can catch a source change).
  3. Before escalating as a fault, rule out the benign cause: a teacher manually switching off one TV group during class (confirmed more than once in Room 1 — the case then closes as manual operation, no fault).
  4. Check the dark display's decoder: online in snapshot? thumbnail live? temperature normal? Route to Case 6 if the decoder itself is the problem.
  5. Report as ABNORMAL with the evidence; propose the least-disruptive remedy and wait for the owner's confirmation.
Do NOT: downgrade a Room 1 case to “check region”, or raise a display fault before checking the manual-operation explanation during class hours.

Case 5 Route down — monitoring path failure High

Recognise it

The watchdog alerts “route down”: two consecutive probe rounds in which both the camera-subnet and AV-processor probes failed. Nothing remote can be seen or checked while it lasts — but the rooms themselves may be perfectly fine.

Handle it — step by step

  1. Do not diagnose room faults from missing data: no patrol/readiness conclusion is valid until the route recovers.
  2. Investigate the path in order: operator's secure remote access → site subnet router → the two AV subnets (172.18.2.0/24 and 172.18.22.0/24).
  3. Distinguish a full route failure (both subnets) from a single-device failure (one probe only — that is context, not this case).
  4. When a probe round succeeds again, the watchdog reports “route recovered”; resume normal schedules and note the gap in the day's reports.
  5. If the outage will cover the 09:00 class start, inform the owner promptly — on-site support may be needed because remote readiness cannot be verified.
Do NOT: mark rooms faulty, or re-run missed device actions, because monitoring was blind.
Experience note: the 2026-10-12 subnet outage is why the watchdog exists; it alerts only on state change so an outage produces exactly two messages (down, recovered).

Case 6 Decoder normal but thumbnail always 404 — NEEDS-RESTART High

Recognise it

A TV decoder is healthy in the snapshot data (online, normal temperature) but its Manager thumbnail never loads, while other rooms' thumbnails work.

Handle it — step by step

  1. Re-fetch the thumbnail with the arm-and-retry method: first request arms the capture (404 is expected), retry every ~0.5 s up to 10 times. Requests must not send Connection: close — the Manager answers those with 404 every time.
  2. If the image arrives on any retry, it was capture warm-up — not a case. Record nothing.
  3. If it is still 404 after the full pass and the decoder is normal in the snapshot, classify the decoder NEEDS-RESTART.
  4. Escalate to the owner with the evidence before any restart.
  5. On confirmation, restart that one decoder via P3000 #RESET (no login needed) and verify the thumbnail recovers.
Do NOT: report a first-attempt 404 as a fault, or restart before escalating — this exact misread caused false alarms until the arm-and-retry behaviour was proven.
Experience note: proven on SM-11-01 TV-DEC-1 — after its P3000 reset the thumbnail recovered. The root cause of the false 404s was on our side (request style + capture arming), not the Manager.

Case 7 TVs restart on source change / occasional KDS signal loss Medium

Recognise it

Fujitsu IW862/IW982 TVs (Rooms 1, 2, 7, 8) restart or lose the picture when the source changes (PC ↔ laptop, class changeover), or a decoder briefly loses its signal (視信來源終止 followed by 視訊來源恢復 in the logs).

Handle it — step by step

  1. Confirm the site video rule everywhere in the chain: 1920×1080 at 50 Hz on the PC and the AVoIP devices. 60 Hz is a known trigger for unstable video on these TVs.
  2. Pull the decoder log evidence for the window (the 2-hourly captures usually already hold it) and note whether the loss re-locks by itself.
  3. Check the decoder's temperature and online state to rule out a hardware fault (route to Case 6/2 if abnormal).
  4. Track the case in the problem-investigation file: room, time, source-change context, log excerpt. Do not improvise configuration changes.
  5. The agreed mitigation candidate — raising the TV decoders' display-behavior delays (sleep/shutdown to ~300 s, wake-up stays 0 s) — is applied only when the owner decides.
Do NOT: blame HDCP or re-lock churn — the investigation exonerated both; the trigger is the format transition into the 720p60 standby signal on source change.

Case 8 Decoder stuck in a discovery loop Medium

Recognise it

One TV decoder repeatedly re-discovers / re-locks in its log while its neighbours are stable; the room's picture may flicker or drop in a repeating pattern.

Handle it — step by step

  1. Confirm from the log captures that the looping unit is a single decoder and note its loop rate.
  2. Check the decoder's channel assignment in the Manager: the loop occurs if and only if at least one of its channel-select extension families is unassigned (“null”) — the USB family is the usual one.
  3. Report the finding to the owner with the exact unit and the null families found.
  4. Remedy (owner performs or directs): assign the null extension families for that decoder in the Manager. The loop stops within seconds — no reboot needed.
  5. Verify in the next log capture that the loop is gone, and record the unit as fixed.
Do NOT: factory-reset the decoder (a reset can also re-enable the CEC gateway — see the SOP's known issues) or change the Manager's channel scheme, which is correct.
Experience note: proven on SM-11-03 TV1 — loop stopped within seconds of the assignment. Remaining looping units are fixed the same way whenever the owner chooses.

Case 9 USB error storm / decoder log full (“No space left on device”) Medium

Recognise it

A TV decoder's log fills with USB error lines (bursts of dozens), sometimes with repeated “No space left on device” errors, typically starting right after a source re-lock while the system is on.

Handle it — step by step

  1. Identify the unit and the storm window from the log captures; confirm the picture and teaching are unaffected (they usually are).
  2. Continue observation only: track whether the storm is still running at each 2-hourly capture.
  3. Do not clear the log. A LOG-ACTION 4 clear was evaluated and explicitly not approved: the log refills while the storm runs, so clearing destroys evidence without fixing anything.
  4. The durable remedy is an on-site physical USB-path field check for that decoder (cable, port, connected device) — schedule it with the owner if the storm persists.
  5. Report new storm units in the daily summary; a known unit still storming is tracked, not re-escalated, unless the picture is affected.
Do NOT: clear logs, reset the decoder as a “fix”, or propose repeated field checks the owner has already deferred — observation is the standing decision.

Case 10 Kramer Manager web interface hangs Medium

Recognise it

The Manager's web UI will not load (TCP connects, the TLS handshake stalls) while devices keep working. Thumbnails and Manager-driven checks are unavailable.

Handle it — step by step

  1. Confirm the hang is the web service only: the Manager still answers the P3000 protocol on TCP 5000.
  2. With the owner's direction, reboot the Manager via P3000 #RESET — no login is required for the protocol.
  3. Wait ~80 s for full recovery (web UI typically back in ~30 s; P3000 answers first). Verify the UI loads and thumbnails return.
  4. Note the incident and the recovery time in the day's report.
Do NOT: try the web-admin credentials on SSH (they are not SSH credentials and the attempt is refused), or treat a dead web UI as a fleet fault — the AVoIP devices keep running.

Case 11 Switch management login suddenly fails Medium

Recognise it

The 22:00 switch cross-check starts reporting a room's switch pair as “empty / non-JSON login” although nothing was changed on our side and the switches are clearly alive.

Handle it — step by step

  1. Probe before concluding: request the switch's HTTP root and look at the raw response. A 301/308 redirect to HTTPS port 4443 with an empty body means the switch now enforces HTTPS management — the “empty login” is the redirect body being parsed.
  2. Check the firmware version and uptime over HTTPS: an upgrade + reboot between the last good check and the first failure is the coherent cause (exactly what happened to the Room 1 pair on 2026-10-20).
  3. Verify the same password logs in over HTTPS 4443 and that serial numbers/model match the baseline — same devices, new firmware, no credential change.
  4. The fetch tooling already handles this: HTTP first, automatic HTTPS-4443 fallback for the whole fetch. If a new pair shows the same symptom, confirm the fallback engaged before reporting a failure.
  5. Record the data caveats for upgraded pairs (satellite buffered log may return a single line; master logging level may read 5 instead of 6) — caveats, not faults.
Do NOT: report a password rotation, ask for new credentials, or change the switch back to HTTP management — that device-side change is the owner's call only.

Case 12 Late wake — PC source arrives late, room READY No action — record only

Recognise it

A room's PC encoder showed NO SIGNAL in readiness Stage 1 (08:15) but feeds a live image and is judged READY by Stage 2 (08:20). The PC simply woke late.

Handle it — step by step

  1. Record it as late wake （遲醒） in the Stage 2 report: the room's row in the Late wake column, the summary line, and the data file.
  2. Record the wake bracket honestly: last NO SIGNAL time → first IMAGE time. Never invent the exact wake moment.
  3. Stop there. Late wake is a tracking record so the pattern becomes countable (which rooms, how late) — nothing else follows from a single occurrence.
Do NOT: mark the room ABNORMAL, escalate it, or apply any remedy. Not late wake: a black PC source (that is Case 3), a room still NO SIGNAL at the verdict (that is NOT READY), or a recovered thumbnail 404. If Stage 1 data is missing, make no late-wake calls that morning.

Case 13 Common Area comes back online unexpectedly Medium — report only

Recognise it

The Common Area is under scheduled down: camera and decoders offline is its normal state and it is normally omitted from patrol reports entirely. This case is the opposite — its camera/devices come back online, the room is in use, lights are on, or a display is active.

Handle it — step by step

  1. Include the Common Area in that patrol report (the one exception to the omission rule) and flag the state as unexpected.
  2. Report what is actually seen (devices online, lights, display, people) without judging it a fault — a deliberate power-up for an event is the likely explanation.
  3. Take no device action. If the state persists across runs and is unexplained, mention it to the owner in the report's attention line.
Do NOT: switch anything off “back to scheduled down”, or report the normal offline state as a fault in any run.
